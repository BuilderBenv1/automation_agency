# Social Media Operating System — The Automation Agency

**Owner:** Ben Horne
**Time budget:** 1–2 hours a week
**Channels:** LinkedIn · Facebook page · Facebook groups (Instagram cut on purpose — see below)
**Goal:** stay consistently visible to UK business owners, and turn that into Quick-Audit submissions and calls — without a content team.

---

## 1. The system in one line

**Write one thing a week. Reshape it three ways. Screenshot what you already have. Glance at the numbers monthly.**

That's the whole engine. Everything below is detail on how to run it without it eating your week.

---

## 2. Why text-first, and why not Instagram

You post consistently or you don't post at all — production value is a distant second. So the hero unit is **written**, never recorded. The only visual is a screenshot you already generated doing the work: an n8n canvas, the Slack message an automation fired, a before/after spreadsheet, an inbox that used to be a person's job.

Instagram is cut. Without video it's a text-on-image platform, and text-on-image aimed at B2B automation buyers is the worst return on your list. LinkedIn + Facebook (page + groups) is three real channels you'll actually feed. Add IG back later only if you ever *want* to record — not out of obligation.

---

## 3. The create-once, repurpose model

Each week: one hero idea → three posts, one per surface. Same spine, different clothes.

| Surface | Role | How it's shaped | Feeds it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LinkedIn** | Authority home base. B2B buyers, other founders, referrers. | The full written breakdown. Hook first line, short paragraphs, a light question or CTA at the end. | The hero, near-verbatim. |
| **Facebook page** | Friendlier, local angle. | Same idea, warmer and shorter. Talk to a Chesterfield/Derbyshire owner, not a CTO. | The hero, softened. |
| **Facebook groups** | Where local buying intent actually lives. | **Reactive, not scheduled.** A value-first answer to a real thread. No link-drop. | Seeded by what you learned making the hero — but posted only where it genuinely helps. |

**Important:** Groups are a *habit*, not a broadcast slot. Don't force this week's hero into a group on a timetable — that reads as spam and gets you removed. Spend ~20 min/week answering 2–3 real questions helpfully. When your hero topic happens to match a live thread, that's your opening.

## 4. The six content pillars (weighted, not equal)

Weightings are per ~12-post month. They're a lean, not a rule.

### 1. Honest take / myth-bust — **weight it up (≈3–4 / month)**
Your moat. "You don't need AI for this. You need a 20-line Zapier." The engineer who tells people when *not* to automate. This pillar sells precisely because it isn't selling.
Example hooks:
- "Half the 'AI automation' pitches I see could be a spreadsheet formula."
- "The most expensive automation is the one that fixes a process you should have deleted."
- "You don't have an AI problem. You have a copy-paste-between-two-apps problem."
- "'Can you build me an AI agent?' Usually no — and here's what you actually need instead."

### 2. Teardown — **your signature (≈2–3 / month)**
A boring manual job → the automation that kills it. Concrete, specific, screenshot of the real thing.
Example hooks:
- "A dental practice was answering the same 6 booking questions by phone all day. Here's the bot that took it over."
- "This client rekeyed every order from their website into Xero by hand. 40 minutes a day, gone."
- "Watch a customer enquiry turn into a CRM record, a Slack ping and a templated reply — with nobody touching it."

### 3. Proof — **ration it (≈1–2 / month)**
Real client outcomes. You only have a few named clients, so slice each win multiple ways (the number, the before-state, the how, the lesson) and don't burn through them.
- Marmadbir — messaging cost down ~65%.
- PlusRooms — an automation replaced a full day of manual work.
- Punthub — runs unattended.
*Get written permission before naming anyone publicly.*
Example hooks:
- "One client's messaging bill dropped 65% after we changed one thing. It wasn't the AI."
- "This automation has run every day for months and nobody's logged in to check it. That's the goal."

### 4. Plain-English explainer — **flex (≈1–2 / month)**
Demystify n8n / chatbots / AI agents for non-technical owners. Overlaps with Teardown — use it when you *don't* have a fresh teardown.
Example hooks:
- "What 'n8n' actually is, explained without a single buzzword."
- "'AI agent' vs 'chatbot' vs 'automation' — what you're actually buying."

### 5. Build-in-public / personal — **≈1–2 / month**
Humanise the solo engineer. What you're building this week, an honest lesson, a thing that broke.
Example hooks:
- "Shipped a WhatsApp booking bot this week. The hard part wasn't the AI — it was the edge case where two people book the same slot."
- "Ten years of writing this stuff and I still start every build by asking 'should this exist at all?'"

### 6. Community spark — **≈1 / month + reactive**
Polls, questions, free-help offers. Cheap reach, warms the audience.
Example hooks:
- "What's the one task you'd pay to never do again? I'll tell a few of you exactly how I'd automate it — free."
- "Poll: what eats the most time in your week — data entry / reporting / chasing people / scheduling?"

## 5. The CTA ladder (leads-weighted, not spammy)

Roughly per month:

- **~70% soft** — engage / save / follow. No ask beyond attention. (Honest takes, explainers, build-in-public.)
- **~25% medium → the Free Quick-Audit.** Your workhorse. *"Send me your worst manual job and I'll tell you exactly what I'd automate."* Low friction, ties straight to the `/quick-audit` tool.
- **~5% hard** — book an audit / starter-build pricing (from £350). Rare on purpose.

Always link the medium CTA with a source tag so you can measure it (see §7).

---

## 6. The weekly + monthly ritual

- **Bi-weekly writing batch (~45–60 min):** write 2 heroes at a time. Fortnightly beats monthly — monthly batching is where quality collapses and the habit quietly dies.
- **Posting:** 3 posts/week from the current hero. Schedule LinkedIn + Facebook page; keep the group action live/reactive.
- **Weekly numbers glance (~15 min):** paste the week's stats into the tracker.
- **Monthly pattern read (~30 min):** which pillars/hooks/CTAs actually drove Quick-Audits — not just likes. Double down on winners, cut losers into the next batch.

Lower the bar per post on purpose: **a 120-word honest take that goes out beats a teardown you didn't finish.**

---

## 7. The learning loop (attribution makes it real)

Likes are vanity. The only metric that pays is **Quick-Audit submissions by channel.** That's now wired in:

- Link the Quick-Audit CTA with a source tag:
  - LinkedIn → `https://www.automation-agency.co.uk/quick-audit?src=li`
  - Facebook page → `?src=fb`
  - Facebook group (only where allowed) → `?src=fbg`
  - The free tool → `?src=tool`
- The form captures `src` and the notification email you receive is subject-tagged, e.g. `Quick-Audit [LinkedIn]: Jane — Acme Ltd`, with a **Source** row at the top.
- So every lead tells you where it came from. Feed that into the monthly read: the channel and pillar combination that produces *leads* wins the next batch.

Unknown/typed-in-directly leads show as **Direct / unknown** — that's fine, it's still signal.
